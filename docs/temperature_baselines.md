# Temperature under the same lens as rainfall (exploratory, not in the submitted paper)

Generated by `scripts/26_temperature_baselines.py` (28 Oct 2026). Every number below is computed by
the script and also written to `results/tables/numbers.json` (keys `temp.*`).

## Question

At the same global temperature, SAI cools low latitudes more than mitigation would and leaves
residual warming at high latitudes. Most language homelands are tropical. Are they cooled more
than land, people or the economy? We use exactly the units, weights and member fields of the
rainfall baselines (script 23), with R = T_S - T_M (SAI minus mitigation; negative = SAI cooler).

**Short answer.** In the ensemble-mean climate, yes, but not robustly, and not more than people.

1. Non-institutional-language homelands are cooled more than land: mean difference -0.15 °C against -0.06 °C, and 72% of homeland is more than 0.1 °C cooler against 59% of land. SAI delivers 9% more local cooling than mitigation over language homelands, 3% more over land.
2. People are cooled at least as much (-0.18 °C; 71% cooler), and the economy somewhat less (-0.12 °C). Non-institutional languages are cooler than people in 20 of 60 members and cooler than the economy in 30.
3. The ranking is not robust. Non-institutional languages are cooler than land in 30 of 60 members, which means three of the six response patterns: within a pattern the cell-level temperature field scales with the member's global cooling, so every member of a pattern ranks the units the same way and member counts move in tens. 4. Within countries there is no difference: non-institutional minus institutional language-in-country units, country fixed effects, +0.002 °C (95% CI -0.007 to +0.012); share cooler +1.4 pp (CI -2.1 to +4.9). As for rainfall, any difference between languages and other units comes from geography.
5. Geography: the land-mean difference is -0.15 °C between 15°S and 15°N, -0.04 °C elsewhere and +0.22 °C north of 55°N (FigT2).
6. Magnitude: per degree of global cooling (member median), non-institutional languages end up 0.10 °C cooler than under mitigation, land 0.03 °C. Mitigation itself cools these homelands by 1.70 °C relative to the no-SAI world (land 2.21 °C), so the extra cooling is a small fraction of the total.
7. Families: 149 of 166 families with at least two languages are cooler in the ensemble mean, but only 28 are cooler in at least 90% of members (FigT3).

## How this compares with rainfall

Rainfall gives a robust distributive signal: language families are more often drier than the economy in all 60 members. Temperature does not: the language advantage over land is real in the ensemble mean and in three patterns, and absent in the other three. In both cases the within-country difference is zero and geography drives the result. For the paper, the existing sentence ("On temperature, non-institutional languages fare like people, not worse") remains accurate; a stronger claim that languages are cooled more than other units would not be robust.
